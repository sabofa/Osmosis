## 1. What This Plan Is Built Around

The career target is **quant trading**, with quant development secondary and general SWE as a floor.

**The floor is doing more work than its placement suggests.** First-year trader comp is real, but "ten-plus earning years" is the survivor curve — attrition in years 1–3 is high. This is why quant development is a genuine second track rather than a fallback, and why the coding work in §9 is not optional even on the trading branch.

**What trading firms actually weight:**

1. **Competition math** — AMC, AIME, USAMO. The highest-signal résumé item, though not a formal requirement. Firms also run their own funnels (Optiver's Delta Dash, IMC's challenge).
2. **Demonstrated quantitative skill under pressure** — Optiver's real filter is its "80-in-8" math test, not GPA.
3. **Math coursework depth**, especially probability and statistics.
4. **School**, but less than assumed — see below.
5. **Coding**, enough to be useful.

### The finding that reframes school choice

Quant hiring runs substantially through **open online assessments**, not campus gatekeeping.

- Jane Street states plainly that it has **no GPA or degree requirement**, and its 2025 intern class came from **over 103 different colleges** across 24 countries. Its own FAQ invites applicants to apply even if the firm doesn't visit their campus.
- Citadel hired 350 interns from over 115,900 applicants across 90 colleges globally — a 0.36% rate, but an open funnel.
- Optiver and Citadel Securities screen largely via open assessments.

**The target-school advantage is concentrated at the top of the funnel** — info sessions, fast-tracks, insight-program invites, quant clubs surfacing deadlines, alumni referrals — rather than acting as a hard résumé gate the way it does in investment banking.

**Practical consequence:** school matters less than assumed, competition results matter more, and they travel with you across the open channel.

### The second skill: passing the assessment, not just getting it

AMC and AIME get a résumé screened in. The assessments themselves test a different skill: probability and expected value, mental arithmetic under a clock (Optiver's 80-in-8, Zetamac), market-making and betting games, combinatorics. **This is the part of the pipeline that is fully school-independent** — it's what makes the open-funnel thesis actually cash out for a student without on-campus recruiting.

**Standing track, low hours, starts December 2026:**

- **Blitzstein's Stat 110** (Harvard, free lectures and problem sets) — work through it before the senior-year probability course, so the course becomes review
- **Jane Street puzzle archive** — one per week
- **Zetamac** — daily, five minutes

Not a fall 2026 item. This is what fills the winter after the AMC.

**Researcher — set aside, with a correction.** Hedge-fund QR (Two Sigma, D.E. Shaw, Citadel LLC) is overwhelmingly PhD-entry. Prop-shop QR (Jane Street, SIG, HRT) hires from undergrad and master's. The "five to six extra years" premise applies to the first category, not the second. Keep it set aside for FIRE reasons, but don't treat the whole category as closed.

**Note on GPA claims:** prep-industry guidance citing 3.7+ thresholds contradicts the firms' own published language. Anecdote, not policy.

## 2. ⚠️ First-Year Status — The Highest-Stakes Unresolved Item

You will graduate with roughly 60–75 college credits. Whether that preserves first-year applicant status determines access to first-year-only merit awards — Stamps, Gold, and the UT Dallas National Merit package.

| School | Transfer threshold | Pre-graduation PSEO credit separate? | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UT Dallas** | Enrollment in a long semester *after* HS graduation | **Yes** — explicitly "first-time freshmen" for admission *and scholarship* | ✅ Protected |
| **UMN Twin Cities** | Enrollment after HS graduation | **Yes** — PSEO/AP students are still freshmen | ✅ Protected |
| **UIUC** | 24+ transferable hours *after* HS graduation | **Yes** — count begins at graduation | ✅ Protected |
| **Purdue** | 12+ credit hours taken *post*-high-school | **Yes** | ✅ Protected |
| **Georgia Tech** | 30 semester hours — **and the transfer-applicant definition explicitly includes dual enrollment** | **Conflicting** | ⚠️ **AT RISK** |
| **UW-Madison** | Under 24 transferable credits = freshman; dual-enrollment coursework counts as transferable | **Ambiguous** | ⚠️ Unclear |

### The Georgia Tech contradiction

GT's transfer-*eligibility* pages say dual enrollment and AP/IB credit **do not** count toward the 30-hour post-graduation requirement. GT's transfer-*applicant definition* page says the count **includes** dual enrollment. These cannot both govern.

GT runs dual enrollment programs and admits dual-enrolled students as first-years, so the likely answer is favorable. "Likely" is not adequate when Stamps and Gold hang on it.

**Why it's urgent, not a summer 2027 item:** if the answer is unfavorable, senior-year credit loading becomes a variable to **control rather than maximize**. That decision happens at registration.

### The trip-wire

**Do not enroll in any college coursework in a long semester after high school graduation.** One such enrollment voids UT Dallas's National Merit deferral, forfeits freshman status at UMN, UIUC, and Purdue, and violates GT's deferral terms. Applies to a gap year, a deferral, and any "just one class to stay sharp" impulse.

## 3. Major Strategy

**Apply as a Mathematics major at direct-admit schools.**

### The admissions differential, quantified

| School | CS admit rate | Math / parent college | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UIUC** (Fall 2025, first-choice major) | **7.4%** (CS in Grainger) | **36.4%** (LAS, houses Math) | **~5x** |
| **Georgia Tech** (Fall 2025, by college) | **9.7%** (Computing) | **14.8%** (Sciences, houses Math) | **~1.5x** |
| Purdue | Not published by major | — | Unknown |

**The GT advantage is modest** — roughly 1.5x at the college level. The strong version of this argument lives at UIUC.

### ⚠️ The UIUC trap

**Mathematics & Computer Science (CS+Math) is housed in LAS but carries CS-level selectivity.** The 36.4% figure is for Mathematics and Statistics proper.

**Apply first-choice Mathematics, second-choice Statistics. Never list anything with "Computer Science" in the name** — Statistics & CS is a CS+X major with the same problem. Listing a CS+X program discards the entire reason for the math strategy.

**The real UIUC vulnerability is the 3.5 unweighted GPA.** UIUC is more formula-driven than GT and weights core-course GPA, and OOS applicants face a stricter bar. Two mitigations:

- The sophomore Bs **were not in math** — math grades were A's. The counselor letter should state the trend explicitly and the additional-information section can carry two lines
- **The SAT decides how much the GPA matters.** A 1500+ with a near-perfect math section makes the 3.5 a footnote. A 1400 makes it the headline. This is why the March 2027 sitting is more load-bearing for UIUC than the calendar suggests

### What math costs

**For quant recruiting: nothing.** No firm in the set prefers CS over math for undergraduate trader or developer roles. Jane Street requires no specific major; IMC lists CS, economics, engineering, and math as equivalent. The admissions advantage is free.

**For generic SWE (the floor): this is where the cost lands.** New-grad SWE screens filter on CS degrees, in a market with entry-level postings down 25–28% and 42.5% underemployment. Survivable with a CS minor and real projects, but no longer automatic.

### The one-way door, confirmed

Internal transfer into CS is restricted-to-blocked at both target schools. GT's College of Computing takes change-of-major only on a limited, space-available basis; students starting Summer 2024 or later lost the ability to switch freely. UIUC states internal CS transfer is typically as selective as or more selective than freshman direct admit.

**Treat the major you apply in as the major you graduate in.**

### GT math major + CS minor — available, not guaranteed

A pure Mathematics major at GT can pursue most CS minors, with two constraints: the **Theory minor is off-limits** to Math majors, and the CS minor is **capacity-limited and application-based** (CS 1331 with a B or higher, good standing, ≥48 credit hours remaining, competitive application window). Take CS 1331 early. Don't treat the minor as insurance you're certain to have.

## 3a. Senior-Year Courses — the Two Open Slots

Confirmed electives: physics with calculus, differential equations, linear algebra. Two slots remain. Fill them with the two highest-return courses on any quant path.

**1. Calculus-based probability.** The single most interview-relevant course available. At UMN, candidates are STAT 3021 (Introduction to Probability and Statistics) or MATH 4653 (Elementary Probability). Verify prerequisites and PSEO availability.

**2. A proof-based course.** Candidates: MATH 2283 (Sequences, Series, and Foundations) or the writing-intensive 3283W. **Applying as a Mathematics major with Calc 1–3, linear algebra, and differential equations but no proof course reads thinner than it looks from the outside** — those are all computational. Proofs are also what AIME and later the Putnam actually run on.

### Major label — settled

Mathematics versus Applied Mathematics does not matter for quant recruiting or for admissions (same LAS pool at UIUC, same College of Sciences at GT). Trading is the role where the major label matters least of the three.

What matters is the transcript *inside* the major: probability, mathematical statistics, stochastic processes, one algorithms course, an analysis sequence. **Pure Mathematics plus a Statistics minor is the cleaner version** because it forces the analysis and leaves the applied courses as choices.

**Do not chase the label. The course list is the lever.**

**Corrected logic:** strong scores argue *against* gapping (NM is live, the file works). Weak scores argue *for* it (test scores are the most improvable number, and NM was already gone at that score).

### If AIME qualification fails (both November sittings under 85)

**Kaggle — primary backup.** Continuous, open, publicly ranked, no qualification. A bronze or silver medal is verifiable, data-based, maps onto quant work, and reuses the detector's skills.

**HiMCM** — if a team exists. No qualification required, and it addresses the solo-profile problem.

**Optiver Delta Dash and IMC's trading challenge** — firm-run competitions that double as recruiting funnels. Check eligibility.

**Codeforces — the quant-dev credential.** Same logic as the AMC: public, ranked, verifiable, travels with you across the open channel. It's also **the only coding signal that cannot be vibecoded**, since contests are timed and observed. Note that quant-dev screens are C++-heavy while scikit-image builds Python. Start in Python for sustainability; move to C++ well before 2030.

### ⛔ Putnam — do not compete as a dual-enrolled high schooler

Four lifetime attempts, and eligibility rules explicitly warn high school entrants about the cap. Burning attempts at 17 costs you shots later when your math is stronger and it counts more. Wait until you're degree-seeking.

### If the detector fails to find users

**Trigger: fewer than 10 artists who have used it more than once, by May 1, 2027.**

**Do not start a new project.** Pivot: **publish the dataset.** A well-documented, ethically sourced, properly credited human-vs-AI art dataset is a real citable artifact, it's the hardest part of the work regardless, and it's literally what the community asked for.

Secondary: provenance and attribution tooling — C2PA metadata checking, watermark detection.

### If open-source contribution stalls

Expected. First PRs get rejected or heavily revised; that *is* the value. If scikit-image doesn't click after two months, switch projects — scikit-learn or a Hugging Face library — not goals.

## 8. Research and Recommendation Letters

### UMN research — the door is different than assumed

**⛔ UROP is explicitly closed to PSEO students.** Eligibility requires enrollment in a degree-seeking undergraduate program; PSEO students are non-degree-seeking.

**✅ Informal and paid research is permitted.** PSEO students may work and volunteer in research positions (excluding work-study roles). The documented route is identifying faculty via **experts.umn.edu** and arranging a position directly.

**Practical difference:** no portal, no deadline, no structure. It happens only if you send individual emails to individual professors — which is why February 2027 is a hard calendar item. UROP opens once you're degree-seeking.

### Letters

- **Georgia Tech requires no letters of recommendation.** It accepts up to four. Optional means non-traditional recommenders carry no penalty
- **Common App accepts college instructors as teacher evaluators.** Dual enrollment professors are explicitly valid academic recommenders
- **The counselor report** is typically completed by the PSEO coordinator or program administrator. GT works with dual enrollment counselors through its Slate.org portal
- **A summer program counselor or instructor can also write a letter.** If a program happens, that letter replaces the research letter
- **A teacher recommendation is required for Ross, PROMYS, and Mathcamp** — another reason instructor relationships start this semester rather than senior year

**What still holds:** letters are optional, but at the Stamps and Gold level — 425–450 semifinalists cut to ~150 on résumé and story — a specific letter does work a transcript can't.

**This semester:** pick one or two instructors. Office hours with real questions, not grade questions. Mention the project out loud. By spring they should be able to describe something specific about you.

**The counselor, separately:** worth repairing regardless, since they control PSAT registration, the transcript, and the school report. The repair is boring — email, show up, state your plans, hand them a one-page summary so their job is easy. Most counselor friction is workload, not dislike.

**The pattern risk:** most items are solo. Stamps and Gold committees explicitly select for leadership and impact, and this file would otherwise read as brilliant and entirely self-contained. The art community, dev friends collaborating on the build, and running a workshop or writing the provenance documentation for real users are the other fixes. Be deliberate about this rather than hoping it emerges.

### Essay strategy

**Do not write the project essay.** The reader already knows about the detector from the activities list and supplement. Re-explaining it spends 650 words restating the file, and by fall 2027 "I built an AI thing" will be among the most saturated archetypes in the pile.

**Write the position instead:** a painter who is training the models he's fighting. Years of making art by hand, a community flooded by generated images, and a response that required generating tens of thousands of AI images personally to teach a model what fakes look like. Becoming fluent in the thing hurting the people you're defending. Underneath it, quieter: leaving art for math, and building a tool so the people staying behind can keep working.

The rest of the application is math-heavy and explains nothing about where that came from. This does structural work nothing else can.

| Piece | Contains |
|---|---|
| Common App essay | The painting, the community, the tension. The detector appears as a consequence of who you are, not as the subject |
| Activities section | The project stated plainly — what it does, who uses it, what you built |
| GT / UIUC supplements | The concrete case: math major, quantitative finance interest, why this school |

**Avoid:** any mention of FIRE or compensation. Coherent reasoning, but it reads badly in a personal essay.

**Drop the B-student-to-4.0 arc as the primary narrative.** True and real, but it's the single most common archetype in admissions and there's now something better. Let the transcript show the trend, let the counselor letter mention it (**specifically that the Bs were not in math**), use the additional information section if needed. A couple of lines, then move on.

**Timing:** write the real version in **summer 2027**, after the project has met real users.

**Starting now: keep a notes file.** When an artist says something about AI images, record it verbatim. When the detector confidently flags a human painting, write down how that felt. These cannot be reconstructed later.
